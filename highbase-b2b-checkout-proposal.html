    Principles behind the fixes

    Total cost, from the first screen

    Delivery, VAT and discounts are visible in the cart and stay pinned through checkout. No number changes between screens.

    The supplier is the unit

    Minimums, fees, coupons, dispatch and delivery windows all belong to a supplier group, and the cart is shaped that way.

    Verify the buyer, not the order

    Trade documents live on the account and are collected once. Paperwork can delay dispatch; it can never block placing an order.

    Repeat buying is the default

    SKU entry, saved lists and recurring baskets treat the second order as the main event, not the first.

    Speaks procurement

    PO numbers, cost centres, reclaimable VAT, proforma invoices and Net 30 terms — the vocabulary a finance team already uses.

    Legible and reachable

    4.5:1 minimum contrast, visible focus rings, real labels, tabular figures, keyboard-operable steppers, and full RTL support via logical properties.

    Method and limits

    This is an expert inspection by a single evaluator, not a usability test — it predicts where buyers will struggle, it does not measure that they do. Nielsen's own guidance is that one evaluator finds roughly a third of the problems in an interface, so treat the count as a floor. Severity here weighs frequency, impact and persistence, but no evaluator-agreement pass has been run, so ratings are one considered opinion. The findings worth validating with real buyers first are A2 (documents inside the order) and P1 (receipt upload on a separate page), because both trade against compliance and finance constraints this evaluation cannot see.

    Deliberately out of scope: catalogue and product pages, supplier onboarding, the credit-terms application, returns, and the buyer's order-history area. Arabic copy in this prototype is illustrative — the RTL toggle demonstrates layout mirroring, not final translation.
