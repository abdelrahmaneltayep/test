What this proposal changes, and why

Findings come from the recorded walkthrough of qa.highbasemarket.com (cart → review → address-confirmation → order placed) and the two confirmation screenshots. Each fix maps to an established e-commerce or B2B procurement practice, not to taste.

4 → 3screens from cart to paid
3 → 0document uploads inside the order
Screen 2 → 1where the delivery fee first appears
19issues addressed

Cart

#What happens todayChangeWhy
1One flat product grid, though minimums, delivery and coupons are all per supplier.Group the cart by supplier, with a header carrying that supplier's terms.Marketplace carts must mirror how the order will actually split, or the buyer can't predict cost or delivery.
2"Minimum order for this product supplier: BHD 10.000. Add BHD 6.000 more…" repeated on every card in the group.One progress bar per supplier showing amount reached, target and exact gap.Repetition of an identical message trains buyers to ignore it. Progress toward a threshold both informs and motivates.
3"18pcs" sits next to "BHD 1.000" with no per-piece figure.Show price per case and per piece on every line, plus total pieces.Procurement buyers compare cost per unit across pack sizes. Doing that arithmetic for them is the core job of a B2B catalogue.
4"Only 1 item in stock" shown while quantity stays at 7 and the line total still calculates.Flag the conflict on the line, suppress the total, offer "Reduce to 1" and a restock alert.An error the system can fix should come with the fix attached. Surfacing it later, at payment, costs the whole order.
5Order Summary shows Subtotal = Total. A BHD 10.000 delivery fee first appears on the next page.Itemise delivery, coupons and VAT in the cart summary.Unexpected extra cost at checkout is the most cited reason for abandonment in every published cart study.
6One supplier under its minimum stalls the whole basket.Check out the ready suppliers now; keep the rest in the cart.Partial fulfilment converts today's revenue instead of deferring all of it.
7No reorder, saved list or SKU entry.Quick-add by SKU, saved lists, "save as recurring basket".B2B demand is repetitive. The winning flow for a repeat order skips browsing entirely.

Checkout

#What happens todayChangeWhy
8A panel labelled "Step / Address & Documents", plus a "Step 2" chip lower down. No count, no sequence.A three-step indicator: Cart → Delivery & details → Payment.Progress indicators reduce abandonment only when they show position and remaining length.
9CR number, commercial licence and personal ID are uploaded inside the order, before Place Order.Verify the business once at account level; checkout shows a verified chip. Unverified buyers can still order — dispatch waits, not the order.Compliance is a property of the buyer, not the basket. Repeating it per order taxes exactly the customers who buy most often.
10The address-and-documents page contains no order summary at all.Sticky summary with the full breakdown on every checkout screen.Nobody should commit to a payment they cannot see.
11No delivery date, window or shipment count.Day and time-slot pickers per shipment, with capacity shown.Businesses staff their goods-in. Chosen windows cut failed deliveries and redelivery cost.
12No PO number, cost centre or invoice recipient.Optional PO, cost centre and invoice email, printed on the tax invoice.Without them the buyer re-keys the order into their own finance system.
13Bank transfer is the only route, discovered after the order is placed.Payment method chosen before commitment; Net 30 shown with an application path.Credit terms are the main reason a trade buyer picks one marketplace over another.
14"Place Order" with no amount anywhere on screen."Place order · BHD 11.000".The amount on the commit control is the cheapest reassurance available.
15Field values rendered in orange on white (Bahrain, Manama, 18) — roughly 2:1 contrast, and orange reads as a warning.Values in near-black at 17:1; orange reserved for genuine attention states.WCAG 1.4.3 requires 4.5:1 for body text, and colour must not carry a meaning it doesn't have.

Confirmation & payment

#What happens todayChangeWhy
16"Click on Track Your Order to upload the transaction receipt" — the payment step ends on a different page.Receipt upload sits directly under the bank details.Every navigation hop between a task and its completion loses users.
17No payment deadline and no stated consequence.A stock-reservation window with a live countdown and a named expiry.Manual bank transfer needs a deadline to convert; without one it becomes "later".
18The uploaded ID renders full-bleed at natural size, pushing Place Order far below the fold.Attachments render as fixed-height rows: thumbnail, name, size, preview, remove.User content must never dictate page layout.
19The confirmation ends at bank details — no delivery date, no next steps.Dated status timeline through to invoicing, plus supplier contact and proforma download."When does my stock arrive" is the only question the buyer has after paying.

Principles applied

💰 Total cost, from the first screen

Delivery, VAT and discounts are visible in the cart and stay pinned through checkout. No number changes between screens.

🏭 The supplier is the unit

Minimums, fees, coupons, dispatch and delivery windows all belong to a supplier group, and the cart is shaped that way.

📋 Verify the buyer, not the order

Trade documents live on the account and are collected once. Paperwork can delay dispatch; it can never block placing an order.

🔁 Repeat buying is the default

SKU entry, saved lists and recurring baskets treat the second order as the main event, not the first.

🧾 Speaks procurement

PO numbers, cost centres, reclaimable VAT, proforma invoices and Net 30 terms — the vocabulary a finance team already uses.

♿ Legible and reachable

4.5:1 minimum contrast, visible focus rings, real labels, tabular figures, keyboard-operable steppers, and full RTL support via logical properties.

Not covered here

Deliberately out of scope for v1: catalogue and product pages, supplier onboarding, the credit-terms application, returns, and the buyer's order-history area. Arabic copy in this prototype is illustrative — the RTL toggle demonstrates layout mirroring, not final translation.
